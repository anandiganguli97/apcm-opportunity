APCM Readiness Atlas

APCM pays primary care practices a monthly fee for each patient, and the top tier, worth the most, is for low-income patients enrolled in both Medicare and Medicaid (Qualified Medicare Beneficiaries, or QMBs). This atlas asks which practices already run the care management the codes require, and whether the practices that see the most QMB patients are among them.

Methods and limits

How this was built

  • Primary care clinicians are physicians with a CMS provider type of family practice, internal medicine, general practice or geriatric medicine, plus nurse practitioners and physician assistants who billed an annual wellness visit that year.
  • Practices come from the current CMS Doctors and Clinicians file (the organization each clinician is listed with most often). Clinicians with no organization count as solo practices.
  • Visible billing means a clinician-code row in the CMS Physician & Other Practitioners file. CMS drops rows for fewer than 11 patients, so small volumes are invisible, not zero.
  • QMB patients are estimated: each clinician's dual-eligible count times the county share of dual enrollees who are QMBs (CMS Medicare Monthly Enrollment). The public files do not flag QMBs per clinician.
  • Readiness tiers: multi-service means CCM or PCM plus BHI, collaborative care, remote monitoring or TCM; visit-based means only TCM, advance care planning or wellness visits.
  • Opportunity uses CY2026 national rates (RVUs times the conversion factor, no geographic adjustment) and the traditional Medicare count from the Geographic Variation file. The share of patients with two or more chronic conditions, capture and consent are scenario assumptions, shown in the table above.
  • The forecast ranks practices by a logistic model trained on how practices took up G2211, a primary care code launched in January 2024. It is a ranking, not an APCM uptake rate, and stays a draft until it is frozen before 2025 data are published.
  • Not covered: Medicare Advantage, FQHC and RHC billing under their own payment systems, and anything that needs patient-level claims. A recent survey of primary care clinicians found 14.9% self-reported APCM use (Grumbach et al., JGIM, October 2026); this atlas is the national claims-based complement.
  • Code, models and tests: github.com/anandiganguli97/apcm-opportunity.
