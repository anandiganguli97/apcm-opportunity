## Page 1 — The opportunity

### What changed

Medicare began paying for Advanced Primary Care Management (APCM) on January 1, 2025. The monthly bundle is intended to support longitudinal primary care work, with higher tiers for patients with greater medical and social complexity. CMS describes a base tier, a tier for patients with at least two qualifying chronic conditions, and the highest tier for Qualified Medicare Beneficiaries (QMBs) who also meet the chronic-condition threshold. The service includes consent, a primary-care focal point, urgent access, a patient-centered care plan, care coordination and population management.

## Page 2 — Risks and recommended moves

### Three moves for leadership

1. **Confirm operational readiness.** Map how patients receive 24/7 urgent access, how care plans are created and shared, who owns follow-up after transitions, how consent is recorded, and how population gaps are identified. Historical CCM/TCM/BHI billing is a useful signal, but it does not prove every APCM requirement is met.
2. **Pilot the economics.** Select a small cohort, review fee-schedule rates and same-month concurrent billing restrictions with compliance, and track staff effort, consent, eligible months, rejected claims, net revenue and patient liability. Compare the observed result to low/base/high projections before scaling.
3. **Protect equity in implementation.** Stratify outreach, staffing and conversion by QMB share, rurality and shortage-area status. Validate that source data include FQHC/RHC settings and explain which Medicare Advantage and suppressed populations are not observed.

### Risks to interpret before acting

- Diagnosis-based chronic-condition flags are proxies and may not satisfy clinical durability / risk criteria.
- Public provider-level counts suppress small cells and cannot identify patient-month conflicts.
- Fee-for-service files omit Medicare Advantage; institutional data and safety-net billing pathways require separate coverage checks.
- A billing NPI can hide incident-to staff and group workflows.
- The practice readiness score is a transparent proxy from prior billing, not an audit of care plans, access, staffing or consent.
- APCM adoption research has already appeared online; benchmark against that work before claiming a novel market forecast.
