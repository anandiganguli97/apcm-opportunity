## Relevant studies

1. Reddy et al. “Use of Chronic Care Management Among Primary Care Clinicians.” *Annals of Family Medicine* 18(5):455–457 (2020), DOI [10.1370/afm.2573](https://doi.org/10.1370/afm.2573). Predecessor-code utilization; useful baseline for adoption patterns, not a direct APCM readiness validation.
2. “Informing Advanced Primary Care Management: Trends From Care Coordination Among Medicare Beneficiaries in 2020–2023.” *The Permanente Journal* (2025), DOI [10.7812/TPP/25.040](https://doi.org/10.7812/TPP/25.040). Maps pre-APCM care coordination trends and provider mix.
3. Grumbach et al. “Primary Care Clinician Uptake of Medicare Advanced Primary Care Management Billing Codes.” *Journal of General Internal Medicine*, online 2026-10-22, DOI [10.1007/s11606-026-10949-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-026-10949-8). A survey of 404 primary care clinicians; 14.9% reported using APCM. The closest prior work.

## Positioning

This project does not claim to be the first APCM uptake study. Grumbach et al. measure self-reported use in a clinician survey. This project is the national claims-based complement: it uses every traditional Medicare primary care clinician in the CMS public files, measures pre-APCM readiness from billing rather than self-report, estimates where QMB patients (the G0558 population) get care relative to that readiness, and pre-registers a practice-level forecast that the 2025 claims file will test. The two designs answer different questions and can check each other: a survey reaches clinicians' intentions and barriers; claims show what was billed, but only above CMS's 11-beneficiary threshold.
